With the Influence action, you urge a creature to do something. Describe or roleplay how you’re communicating with the monster. Are you trying to deceive, intimidate, amuse, or gently persuade? The DM then determines whether the monster feels willing, unwilling, or hesitant due to your interaction; this determination establishes whether an [[Ability Checks|ability check]] is necessary, as explained below.

**_Willing._** If your urging aligns with the creature’s desires, the creature wants to fulfil your request in a way it prefers.

**_Unwilling._** If your urging is repugnant to the creature or counter to its alignment, the creature doesn’t want to comply.

**_Hesitant._** If you urge the creature to do something that it is hesitant to do, it may partially comply with your commands or be indifferent to your requests. 

The DC for the check is determined by the DM and is influenced by the creature’s attitude toward you, the risk or cost of the request, and how well your approach fits the situation. Before the roll, the DM should make clear what success and failure are likely to mean.  

A successful check doesn’t guarantee that a creature does exactly what you want. Instead, it shifts the creature’s response in a reasonable way. For example, a successful check might not convince an unwilling, hostile guard to surrender, but it might make the guard willing to hear you out, delay an attack, lower their weapon, or allow a further attempt to negotiate.
