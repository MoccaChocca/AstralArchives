When you take the Hide action, you make a Dexterity (Stealth) check in an attempt to hide, following the rules for hiding. If you succeed, you become [[hidden]].

# Hiding
The DM decides when circumstances are appropriate for hiding. When you try to [[hide]], make a [[Dexterity|Dexterity (Stealth)]] check. Until you are discovered or you stop hiding, that check's total is contested by the [[Wisdom|Wisdom (Perception)]] check of any creature that actively searches for signs of your presence.

You can't [[hide]] from a creature that can see you clearly, and you give away your position if you make noise, such as shouting a warning or knocking over a vase. An [[invisible]] creature can always try to [[hide]]. Signs of its passage might still be noticed, and it does have to stay quiet.

In combat, most creatures stay alert for signs of danger all around, so if you come out of hiding and approach a creature, it usually sees you. However, under certain circumstances, the DM might allow you to stay [[hidden]] as you approach a creature that is distracted, allowing you to gain [[Advantage and Disadvantage|advantage]] on an attack roll before you are seen.

***Passive Perception.*** When you [[hide]], there's a chance someone will notice you even if they aren't searching. To determine whether such a creature notices you, the DM compares your [[Dexterity|Dexterity (Stealth)]] check with that creature's [[Passive Checks|passive Wisdom (Perception)]] score.

**What Can You See?** One of the main factors in determining whether you can find a [[hidden]] creature or object is how well you can see in an area, which might be [[Vision|lightly]] or [[Vision|heavily obscured]].
