A band of adventurers sneaks up on a bandit camp, springing from the trees to attack. A gelatinous cube glides down a dungeon passage, unnoticed by the adventurers until the cube engulfs one of them. In these situations, one side of the battle might surprise the other.  
The DM determines who is surprised. If neither side tries to be stealthy, the creatures automatically notice each other. Otherwise, the DM compares the [[Dexterity|Dexterity (Stealth)]] checks of any creatures [[Hide|hiding]] with the passive [[Wisdom|Wisdom (Perception)]] score of each creature on the opposing side. Any creature that doesn’t notice a threat is surprised at the start of the encounter.

When combat starts, creatures roll [[initiative]] as normal. During the first round of combat, a surprised creature can’t move or take actions.

Any creature who is not surprised may take one [[Action]] on their turn during the surprise round. You have [[Advantage and Disadvantage|advantage]] on attacks and other checks made against surprised creatures. A member of a group can be surprised even if the other members aren't.
