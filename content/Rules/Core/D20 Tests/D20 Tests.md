When the outcome of an action is uncertain, the game uses a d20 roll to determine success or failure. These rolls are called D20 Tests.

There are three kinds of D20 Test:
- [[Ability checks]]
- [[Attack rolls]]  
- [[Saving throws]]

If a rule affects D20 Tests, it affects all three of these rolls. The DM determines whether a D20 Test is needed in a given situation.

The target number for a D20 Test is called a Difficulty Class, or DC. The DC for an attack roll is usually the target’s Armor Class, or AC.

## Degrees of Success
Sometimes it is useful to know not only whether a creature succeeds or fails on a Test, but also how strongly they succeed or fail. In these cases, the DM can use degrees of success to help determine the outcome.  

The DM decides whether degrees of success apply before or after a roll, depending on the situation. They are most useful when a task has variable outcomes, such as gathering information, navigating dangerous terrain, influencing a creature, crafting an item, or attempting a risky physical feat.

Degrees of success are measured against the DC of the Test. If a creature meets or exceeds the DC, it succeeds. If it rolls above or below the DC by a meaningful margin, the DM can adjust the result to reflect the strength of the success or failure.

The margin required for a greater success or greater failure depends on the task. A simple check might have only two outcomes: success or failure. A more complex or risky check might have several possible degrees, with higher results producing better outcomes and lower results producing worse ones.

For example, a successful [[Charisma|Charisma (Persuasion)]] check might convince a guard to let the characters pass. A much higher result might also make the guard friendly, encourage them to share useful information, or prevent them from reporting the encounter. A much lower result might make the guard suspicious, hostile, or more likely to call for help in future encounters.

Degrees of success do not create automatic critical successes or critical failures. They are a tool the DM can use when the margin of a roll would reasonably affect the outcome.
