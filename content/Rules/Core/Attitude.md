A creature’s attitude reflects its general disposition toward a character. Attitude is a summary of how the creature currently views that character, not a complete description of its personality, motives, or beliefs.
  
The DM adds any necessary nuance based on the creature’s history, ideals, goals, fears, and relationship with the character. A creature’s attitude can change over time in response to the story, the character’s actions, and the outcome of social interactions.

Player characters don’t have attitudes unless their players choose to use them. A player can roleplay an interaction with another player character, and the players involved can use [[Ability Checks|ability checks]] if they want a mechanical sense of how persuasive, deceptive, intimidating, or charming a character is. However, each player decides how their own character responds.

The general attitudes a creature can have toward a character are described below.

#### Helpful
A helpful creature actively wants to aid the character. It usually accepts reasonable requests from that character and may offer assistance without being asked. A helpful creature might accept some risk, cost, or inconvenience to support the character, but it won’t usually act against its own deeply held goals, loyalties, or survival.

#### Friendly
A friendly creature likes the character or views them favourably. It is likely to accept simple, safe, and low-cost requests from that character. A friendly creature might offer advice, minor aid, or useful information, but it usually needs a stronger reason to accept risk, spend significant resources, or go against its own interests.

#### Indifferent
An indifferent creature has no strong feelings toward the character. It might accept a request if doing so is easy, beneficial, expected, or socially appropriate, but it has no special desire to help. Most creatures begin as indifferent toward strangers.

#### Unfriendly
An unfriendly creature dislikes, distrusts, or resents the character. It is unlikely to accept requests from that character unless given a compelling reason, such as payment, leverage, duty, fear, or clear personal benefit. An unfriendly creature generally avoids helping the character and may interpret or vocalise their actions negatively.

#### Hostile
A hostile creature actively opposes the character. It might attack, threaten, obstruct, mislead, report, or sabotage the character, depending on the situation. A hostile creature won’t accept requests from the character unless forced, deceived, compelled, or given an overwhelming reason to do so.
