Many spells and features affect an area rather than a single target. An area of effect usually uses one of six shapes: cone, cube, cylinder, line, Sphere, or emanation.
  
Each area of effect has a **point of origin**, which is the place where the effect begins. Each shape explains where its point of origin is placed.
  
An area of effect can be blocked by [[Cover|Total Cover]]. If every straight line from the point of origin to a location is blocked by [[Cover|Total Cover]], that location isn’t included in the area.
  
If a creature creates an area of effect at a point it can’t see, the effect can’t pass through [[Cover|Total Cover]] to reach that point. If an obstruction, such as a wall, door, floor, or ceiling, blocks the path to the chosen point, the point of origin appears at the closest unblocked point between the creator and the obstruction instead.

### Cone
A Cone extends from its point of origin in a direction chosen by its creator. The Cone widens as it extends. At any point along the Cone, its width equals its distance from the point of origin.  
  
For example, a Cone is 15 feet wide at a point 15 feet from its origin.  
  
The effect that creates the Cone determines its maximum length.  
  
A Cone’s point of origin isn’t included in the area unless its creator chooses to include it.

### Cube
A Cube extends from a point of origin placed anywhere on one face of the Cube. The effect that creates the Cube determines the length of each side.  
  
A Cube’s point of origin isn’t included in the area unless its creator chooses to include it.

### Cylinder
A Cylinder extends from a point of origin placed at the centre of either the top or bottom of the Cylinder. The effect that creates the Cylinder determines its radius and height.  
  
A Cylinder’s point of origin is included in the area.

### Line
A Line extends from a point of origin in a straight path along its length and covers an area defined by its width. The effect that creates a Line specifies its length and width.

A Line’s point of origin isn’t included in the area of effect unless its creator decides otherwise.

### Sphere
A Sphere extends in straight lines from a point of origin outward in all directions. The effect that creates a Sphere specifies the distance it extends as the radius of the Sphere.

A Sphere’s point of origin is included in the Sphere’s area of effect.

### Emanation
An Emanation extends in straight lines from a creature or an object in all directions. The effect that creates an Emanation specifies the distance it extends.

An Emanation moves with the creature or object that is its origin unless it is an instantaneous or a stationary effect.

An Emanation’s origin (creature or object) isn’t included in the area of effect unless its creator decides otherwise.
